Visiting Poland

Public toilets in Poland: a practical guide for visitors

Chcę Siku! editorial teamPublished: 27 October 20264 min read
At a glance

When you need a public toilet in Poland, look for WC or toaleta, check nearby map listings and confirm the entrance, hours and any fee. Chcę Siku! can help you search from your phone's location. Its interface is currently Polish, so the phrases and filter translations below are useful for visitors who do not speak Polish.

Useful Polish words for finding a toilet

Toaleta means toilet or restroom; toalety is the plural. WC is another useful label to look for. If you need to ask someone, you can show them one of these phrases on your phone:

  • Gdzie jest najbliższa toaleta? - Where is the nearest toilet?
  • Czy mogę skorzystać z toalety? - May I use the toilet?
  • Czy toaleta jest bezpłatna? - Is the toilet free to use?
  • Czy można płacić kartą? - Can I pay by card?
  • Czy jest przewijak? - Is there a baby changing table?
  • Czy do toalety można wejść bez schodów? - Can I reach the toilet without stairs?

When asking for a bathroom, naming the feature you need can prevent a second trip. A baby changing table, step-free entrance and a standard toilet are different requests.

How to search nearby in Chcę Siku!

Open the app, allow location access and look at Najbliżej Ciebie (Nearest to you). You can browse places without an account. Check the map and open a result to read its available description, photos and comments. Coverage and detail vary, so an empty result list does not prove that there are no toilets in the area.

Select Bezpłatna for places recorded as free, Przewijak for baby changing, Woda for water, or Dla osób z niepełnosprawnościami for locations marked as accessible for disabled people. There are also Parking and Prysznic (Shower) filters. Several active filters must all match, so remove optional ones if they make the results too limited.

For a fuller explanation of the Polish controls, follow our English guide to using the app. Chcę Siku! is available for iPhone and Android.

Check fees and opening hours at the specific location

Do not assume every toilet is free or that one price applies throughout Poland. Check the listing, sign at the entrance or staff's instructions for that facility. If there is a fee, confirm the accepted payment method before relying on a card or a particular coin.

A toilet inside a café, attraction or other venue may have conditions of access. Ask whether you can use it without a purchase or ticket. A map pin is a starting point for finding a place, not a guarantee that you can enter.

Read the toilet's own hours where available. A building, park or station being open does not by itself confirm that its toilet is open. If no hours are recorded, ask for current information rather than assuming around-the-clock access.

Use official local information alongside the app

A municipal website or local tourist information service can provide another source when a map record is incomplete. Search for the place you are visiting and toalety publiczne, meaning public toilets, then look for the city's official site. Check the date and the individual facility's details.

For a concrete example, Warsaw's official tourist website directs visitors to a city toilet map with accessibility, opening-hour and fee information. Warsaw's municipal contact service also explains how to find toilets through its maps and the street signs labelled Mapa WC. These are Warsaw resources; they do not describe access rules in every Polish city.

Travelling with a baby or specific access needs

A changing-table label does not tell you whether the room is accessible to every parent or carer, whether it needs a key or whether you can bring a pushchair inside. Ask those questions before walking to a place with little information. Our baby changing guide offers a practical checklist.

Similarly, a general accessibility label does not confirm door width, transfer space, grab rails or the absence of steps along the whole route. State your specific requirements when asking a venue. A helpful question can be more precise than simply asking for an accessible toilet. See our guide to checking accessible facilities.

Get to the entrance and help the next visitor

Tap Prowadź for the app's map and direction arrow. If you prefer a street-based walking route, use Otwórz w mapach to open the destination in a supported maps app. Check crossings and the final entrance yourself. Arrange mobile data before travelling and do not rely on the app as a fully offline guide.

After your visit, sign in to leave a factual review. An English note about the entrance, payment or a key can be helpful to another visitor, while confirmed facilities improve the listing itself. If the toilet is closed outside its normal hours, update those hours rather than reporting that the place no longer exists.

For preparation that applies beyond Poland, read how to find a toilet in an unfamiliar city.
